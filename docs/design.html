Hexward · design guideline · v2

The pixel system

How Hexward looks, moves and speaks. Everything sits on a 4 px grid, every edge is hard, and every motion tells you something about your PC's safety. Built for the Slint desktop app; the website and Windows notifications follow the same rules.

P-01Principles

Four rules everything follows

4px

One pixel, one grid

Sizes, gaps and offsets are multiples of 4 px. Borders are 2 px (half a pixel). Nothing is blurred, rounded or anti-aliased on purpose.

0 blur

Depth from hard shadows

Raised things cast a solid 4 px shadow down and right. Pressing a button pushes it into its shadow. Glass, glow and gradients are not part of Hexward.

2 cues

State is colour and shape

Every status has a colour and a second cue: a chip word, a stripe, a blinking core, a shake. People who can't tell red from green still read it.

≤ 480ms

Motion means something

Things move to show cause and effect: a scan sweeps, a threat shakes, a fixed item settles. Nothing loops just to decorate, and everything stops with reduced motion.

C-02Colour

Ink, Moss and Oxide

Ink is the night sky the shields watch over. Moss means protected. Oxide is the brand's warm core and never means danger; danger has its own red.

S-04Surfaces

The stepped panel

Every panel has stepped corners (two 2 px steps), a 2 px edge and a faint 2 px highlight along the top. Only the thing you can act on, or the thing that matters most on a screen, is raised with a shadow.

Flat

Lists, settings rows, stats. Most of the app.

Raised

The hero status, dialogs, toasts, a selected scan type.

Raised · threat

The edge takes the status colour. The fill never does.

The window background is Ink with a 2 px dot every 16 px: the grid the shields keep watch on. Empty states use the same dots, denser.

I-05Icons

12 × 12 pixel icons

Drawn by hand on a 12 × 12 grid with a 1 pixel stroke, shown at 18, 24 or 36 px (whole multiples only, so pixels stay square). Icons take the text colour, or Moss when their item is active.

N-07Notifications

Toasts that say what happened

A toast names what Hexward did and what you can do next. The stripe and edge carry the status; the countdown blocks drain while it waits; a threat shakes once and stays until you act.

Rules for Windows notifications

  • They come from Hexward with the pixel shield, never from "Windows PowerShell".
  • The large icon is the shield with its core in the status colour: red for a threat, Moss when it's handled.
  • Threats use the "reminder" style: they stay until dismissed and offer Open Hexward.
  • Clicking a notification brings Hexward to the front on the right screen.
  • Quiet mode holds everything except threats, then sends one summary.
X-08Do / don't
DO
  • Snap every size to the 4 px grid.
  • Say what happened in plain words: "Moved to quarantine".
  • Animate cause and effect: scan, find, settle.
  • Give every status a word as well as a colour.
DON'T
  • Round corners, blur shadows or add gradients.
  • Use Oxide or red for anything that isn't the brand core or a threat.
  • Loop an animation that carries no information.
  • Cut text off inside a toast or a card.
